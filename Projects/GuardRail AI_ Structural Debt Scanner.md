# GuardRail AI: Structural Debt Scanner
**Status:** hypothesis-set | **Hook:** Your AI is shipping. Your architecture is dying. GuardRail makes sure they survive each other.

## 1. Foundation Hypothesis
**Universal Problem:** AI coding assistants produce code that is functionally correct and passes every linter — but violates your architecture. A component exists; it was rebuilt anyway. A pattern is established; it was ignored. A boundary was crossed that shouldn't be. Standard linters are architecture-agnostic: they enforce syntax and style, not your specific composition rules, import boundaries, or component hierarchy. The only tool that catches structural drift today is a senior engineer in code review — who is now reviewing three times more PRs because AI has accelerated output without raising the structural IQ of the code it writes. 'Vibe-coded' PRs accumulate silently as structural debt until a refactor, a performance incident, or a new engineer's onboarding makes the inconsistency expensive.

### Segment 1: Engineering leads, staff engineers, and platform/DevX teams 
**Who:** Engineering leads, staff engineers, and platform/DevX teams at companies with 20–300 engineers who have adopted AI coding tools (GitHub Copilot, Cursor, Claude Code) and are beginning to see the downstream effects: PRs that pass review but violate architectural patterns, components rebuilt instead of reused, module boundaries crossed in subtle ways that only surface during refactors or incidents.

**Anti-Customer:** Teams without an established architecture or component library — GuardRail has nothing to enforce. Solo developers or very early startups where architectural consistency is not yet a constraint. Organizations where 'it runs and tests pass' is genuinely the quality bar. Teams already running extensive Architecture Decision Records (ADRs) and automated ArchUnit-style tests — they've solved the problem with process.

## 2. Unfair Advantage
**Capability:** GuardRail can be built as a GitHub App that integrates into the PR review flow with no changes to developer workflow. The core intelligence is a fine-tuned or RAG-augmented model that ingests the team's existing codebase, extracts structural patterns (import graphs, component hierarchies, naming conventions, composition rules), and uses these as the evaluation criteria for each incoming diff. Unlike linters, the rules are derived from the codebase itself — not written by a human — which means they stay current as the architecture evolves.
**Motivation:** The cost of structural debt is non-linear: it's nearly invisible when it accumulates but exponentially expensive when it surfaces. A staff engineer spending 30% of review time on AI-generated PRs at a 50-engineer company is burning $150K/year in engineering time on pattern-enforcement that could be automated. The problem is accelerating — AI coding tool adoption doubled year-over-year in 2024 and is still climbing, meaning the volume of structurally inconsistent PRs is growing faster than the reviewer bandwidth to catch them.
**Insight:** The reason standard linters fail here is not a data problem — it's a context problem. ESLint knows nothing about your architecture. GuardRail's insight is that the architecture already lives in your codebase: in the patterns of how components are composed, in the import graph, in the naming conventions, in which abstractions are used where. The architecture just needs to be extracted and made queryable. This is a RAG problem, not a rules-writing problem — and that means the enforcement is automatic and self-updating.

## 3. Principles
- Enforce architecture, not aesthetics — flag structural violations, not style preferences that linters already handle
- No false-positive fatigue — precision over recall; a GuardRail comment must always be actionable and accurate
- Self-updating rules — the enforcement model should learn from merged code, not require manual rule maintenance
- Zero workflow disruption — run in CI as a non-blocking reviewer by default; escalate to blocking only when explicitly configured
- Explainability over black box — every flag must cite the pattern it violates and the location in the codebase where the correct pattern lives

## 4. The Click Test
**Riskiest Assumption:** Engineering leads will trust GuardRail's structural violation flags enough to act on them without a senior engineer's second opinion — because if engineers override or ignore GuardRail comments at the same rate they'd ignore a bot, the tool adds ceremony without reducing debt.
**Test Method:** Shadow-run GuardRail on 4 weeks of merged PRs for 3 pilot teams. Present the flagged violations to each team's senior engineer who reviewed those PRs and ask: 'Would you have flagged this? Is the suggested refactor correct?' Measure agreement rate. Separately, measure how often GuardRail flags something the senior engineer missed.
**Success Metric:** Senior engineer agreement rate above 85% on flagged violations. GuardRail catches at least 1 structural violation per week that the human reviewer missed. Zero instances of GuardRail blocking a structurally sound PR (0% false-positive block rate on the pilot teams).
