# AI Use Case Validator
**Status:** hypothesis-set | **Hook:** Stop pitching AI projects you cannot deliver. Know before you commit.

## 1. Foundation Hypothesis
**Universal Problem:** The gap between 'AI opportunity identified' and 'AI initiative that delivers ROI' is where consulting practices lose credibility and enterprises waste millions. There is no systematic, repeatable process for evaluating whether a specific business process is genuinely ready for AI before resources are committed. Consultants rely on inconsistent judgment, frameworks applied differently by every team member, and vendor-supplied feasibility assessments that are structurally biased toward selling. The result: 60-70% of enterprise AI projects fail to deliver promised value — not because AI does not work, but because the wrong use cases got resourced. Every failure erodes the consulting relationship that took years to build.

## 2. Unfair Advantage
**Capability:** The Four A's framework and AI Opportunity Intents can be encoded into a structured scoring engine with weighted criteria by industry vertical — making outputs reproducible across consultants and engagements. The roadmap builder is differentiable because it explicitly models the current manual workflow alongside the AI-augmented future state; most consulting tools only show the destination. The cost-benefit calculator can be pre-calibrated with industry benchmarks by vertical (financial services, healthcare, manufacturing, retail) so outputs are immediately credible to a CFO without a custom research engagement.
**Motivation:** Consulting firms are under enormous pressure to deliver AI initiatives that work. One high-profile AI failure at a major client destroys years of relationship equity and creates principal-level liability. The Validator exists to protect the consultant's credibility as much as to serve the client — that dual motivation is what makes the tool worth paying for.
**Insight:** The AI readiness evaluation market is dominated by two inadequate options: vendor-supplied assessments (biased toward selling) and generic organizational maturity models (too high-level to assess individual process feasibility). The gap is a tool that is explicitly vendor-neutral, operates at the process level rather than the org level, applies a consistent framework across every evaluation, and produces output the consulting team can stand behind in front of a skeptical CFO. No current tool occupies this position.

## 3. Principles
- Vendor-neutral > Any vendor affiliation
- Process-level granularity > Org-level maturity scores
- Defensible scoring > Black-box recommendations
- Decision support > Decision replacement (consultants own the judgment)
- Reproducible output > Bespoke analysis every time

## 4. The Click Test
**Riskiest Assumption:** Management consultants will trust a software tool to structure and score AI feasibility assessments — and will present those scores to clients in a formal deliverable — rather than treating the output as a starting point they immediately override with their own judgment, making the tool invisible in the final work product.
**Test Method:** Build a Google Sheets-based version of the assessment engine and roadmap builder in 3 weeks. Run it with 3 consulting teams on active client engagements — provide white-glove onboarding and run the first assessment alongside them. Measure whether the tool output appears in client-facing deliverables or gets discarded in favor of the team's existing approach.
**Success Metric:** 2 of 3 consulting teams include Validator output — the assessment score, readiness rationale, or roadmap visualization — in a client-facing deliverable. At least 1 team requests to use it on a second engagement unprompted. At least 1 team asks about pricing before the pilot ends.
