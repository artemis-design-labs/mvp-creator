# Verizon My Project Inbox
**Status:** active | **Hook:** Open one inbox and know exactly what you own, what's next, and who else is in it.

## 1. Foundation Hypothesis
**Universal Problem:** Verizon's engineering teams were coordinating critical work through scattered spreadsheets, buried email threads, and outdated tracking tools. With no shared system, projects collided, ownership blurred, and contributors had no dependable way to see what they were responsible for — or who else was involved. The bottleneck was never engineering capability; it was the absence of a single, trustworthy surface for coordination, which quietly taxed every capable team with rework, missed dependencies, and delivery slippage.

### Segment 1: Individual contributors — engineers across multiple projects
**Who:** Software and systems engineers who contribute to several concurrent projects at once. Their work is scattered across tools, and their real responsibilities live in other people's spreadsheets and threads.
**Pain Point:** They can't reliably answer 'what am I on the hook for this week, and who's waiting on me?' They piece it together from email, chat, and status docs, and things fall through the cracks when ownership is implicit.
**Resolution:** A personal inbox that aggregates everything they own or contribute to across projects into one prioritized view, with explicit ownership and clear collaborators — so nothing they're responsible for is invisible.

### Segment 2: Engineering managers & project leads
**Who:** Leads and managers accountable for delivery across teams and projects. They spend disproportionate time reconstructing status and chasing who owns what.
**Pain Point:** Status lives in stale trackers and inboxes, so they manually assemble the picture, discover collisions late, and can't see where ownership is ambiguous until something slips.
**Resolution:** A shared project view built from the same source contributors use, showing real ownership, dependencies, and collisions — so coordination happens by default instead of by heroics.

**Universal Solution:** My Project Inbox is a shared coordination system that gives every Verizon engineer and manager one dependable place to see the projects they own or contribute to, exactly what they're responsible for, what's blocking or dependent on them, and who else is involved. It replaces spreadsheet-and-email sprawl with a single source of coordination truth — personalized per contributor, but shared across the team so everyone works from the same state.
**Anti-Customer:** Small, co-located teams where a daily standup already keeps everyone aligned, and organizations already standardized on a well-adopted, well-governed work platform where the coordination layer isn't the thing breaking down.

## 2. Unfair Advantage
**Capability:** My Project Inbox is built around a per-person inbox model layered on a shared project state: work is aggregated across every project into a single view for each contributor, while ownership, dependencies, and collaborators stay consistent for everyone looking at the same project. It's a coordination layer, not another tracker — it makes responsibility and involvement explicit rather than implied.
**Motivation:** At Verizon's scale, coordination overhead is the tax on otherwise capable teams. Blurred ownership and colliding projects don't just annoy people — they cause duplicated work, missed hand-offs, and slipped delivery on critical initiatives. The cost is measured in engineer hours and lost time-to-market, not just morale.
**Insight:** The teams already had the talent and the tools to do the work. What they lacked was a shared way to see who owned what and who was involved. Coordination clarity — not more tracking features — was the actual product, because the failure mode was social and organizational, not technical.

## 3. Principles
- Clarity of ownership over activity noise — always show who is responsible, explicitly
- One shared truth, personalized per contributor — same project state, a view built around you
- Coordination is the product — surface responsibility and involvement, not vanity tracking
- Meet engineers where they work — the inbox is the daily starting point, not another destination
- Boring reliability > flashy features
- Outcome > Output

## 4. The Click Test
**Riskiest Assumption:** Engineers and leads will adopt My Project Inbox as their daily coordination starting point — trusting it to reflect true project state — instead of falling back to the spreadsheets and email threads they already know, which would leave the tool a stale second copy.
**Test Method:** Pilot with a few engineering teams by making My Project Inbox the coordination surface for a set of active, cross-team projects for eight weeks. Retire the parallel spreadsheets for those projects, and track daily opens, ownership clarity, and coordination breakdowns against the prior baseline.
**Success Metric:** A large majority of contributors open their inbox daily as their first coordination step, ownership-related collisions and 'who owns this?' escalations drop noticeably, and contributors report they can reliably see what they own and who's involved by the end of the pilot.
