# HANDS AI
**Status:** active | **Hook:** Every morning, know which families need you most — and exactly why — and ask your whole caseload anything in plain language.

## 1. Foundation Hypothesis
**Universal Problem:** Caseworkers manage large, complex caseloads of vulnerable families across fragmented systems, where risk is invisible until it has already escalated. They spend their limited time hunting through disconnected records to reconstruct who needs attention, instead of intervening. There is no reliable way to know each morning which families are trending toward a negative outcome, and no fast way to ask a plain-language question of their own data — so families slip through the cracks not for lack of skill, but for lack of visibility and time.

### Segment 1: Frontline caseworkers & home visitors
**Who:** Family support specialists who each carry a caseload of families and are responsible for engagement, appointments, milestones, and recertification deadlines. They are the ones who must decide, every day, where their limited hours go.
**Pain Point:** Risk is buried across fragmented systems and only becomes visible once a family is already in crisis. They can't quickly see who's trending downward or ask a simple question of their caseload without clicking through rigid screens.
**Resolution:** A prioritized morning queue that surfaces the highest-risk families with a plain explanation for each, plus natural-language search across their whole caseload — so their time goes to the families who need it most, earlier.

### Segment 2: Supervisors & program managers
**Who:** Supervisors accountable for outcomes across a team of caseworkers and for program-level performance, deadlines, and compliance.
**Pain Point:** They lack an early, explainable view of where risk is concentrating across the team, and rely on lagging reports that reveal problems only after outcomes have already slipped.
**Resolution:** A team-level view of risk and workload built from the same explainable signals caseworkers see — so supervisors can target support and catch escalation trends before they become outcomes.

**Universal Solution:** HANDS AI is an AI assistant — Emma — that transforms how caseworkers manage complex caseloads. It continuously analyzes participant data to predict which families are at highest risk of negative outcomes and surfaces them before problems escalate. Each morning Emma delivers a prioritized queue of families needing attention with a clear explanation for every flag, and caseworkers can ask questions of their entire caseload in natural language instead of navigating fragmented systems — turning reactive record-keeping into proactive, focused care.
**Anti-Customer:** Programs with caseloads small enough that a caseworker can hold every family in their head and review them manually, and organizations without integrated participant data for Emma to analyze — where there simply aren't enough signals to predict risk or search across.

## 2. Unfair Advantage
**Capability:** HANDS AI pairs two purpose-built capabilities: a risk-prediction engine that scores every family from engagement patterns, approaching deadlines, and behavioral signals and explains why each is flagged; and a natural-language search layer that interprets a caseworker's plain-language question and returns precisely relevant results across otherwise fragmented systems. It is designed for the specific shape of social-services casework — explainable, prevention-first, and query-driven — not a generic CRM bent to fit.
**Motivation:** In this work, an unseen risk isn't a missed KPI — it's a child's missed recertification, a family that disengages, an outcome that could have been prevented. Caseworkers are capable and overloaded; the scarce resource is attention. Directing that attention to the right families earlier is the difference between reacting to crises and preventing them.
**Insight:** The signals that predict a family's negative outcome are already flowing through the data — missed appointments, dropping engagement, deadlines quietly approaching. They were just never surfaced as a prioritized, explained queue, and never made answerable in plain language. Caseworkers were spending their expertise on hunting and navigating instead of on helping.

## 3. Principles
- Explain every flag — a family is never surfaced without a clear, human-readable reason
- Prevention over reaction — surface risk before it escalates, not after
- Meet caseworkers in plain language — ask questions the way you'd ask a colleague
- Emma prioritizes, the caseworker decides — the assistant recommends, the human judges
- Protect the family — dignity, privacy, and fairness in every prediction
- Outcome > Output

## 4. The Click Test
**Riskiest Assumption:** Caseworkers will trust Emma's risk queue and explanations enough to actually reprioritize their day around it — rather than dismissing the scores and working their caseload the way they always have — and the flagged families will genuinely be the ones heading toward negative outcomes.
**Test Method:** Run Emma's prioritized queue for a cohort of caseworkers over eight to twelve weeks alongside business-as-usual, logging which families were flagged and why, which flags caseworkers acted on, and the outcomes for flagged vs. unflagged families — with caseworkers always making the final call.
**Success Metric:** Flagged families are meaningfully more likely to have been heading toward a negative outcome, caseworkers act on the queue on most mornings, and earlier intervention on flagged families measurably improves engagement or prevents missed deadlines versus the baseline.
